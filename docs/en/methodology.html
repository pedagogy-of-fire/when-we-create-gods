EDITORIAL TRANSPARENCY

Methodological note

This project was developed by a human author who chose not to sign the public edition, in extensive collaboration with OpenAI language models.

What human–AI collaboration means here

Artificial intelligence participated in research, source organization, criticism, hypothesis formation, rewriting, comparison of arguments, editorial structure, technical development of the site, and consistency review. Much of the work was iterative: proposals were challenged, corrected, rewritten, and sometimes abandoned.

This participation does not make the AI system a primary source for empirical claims. Factual assertions should remain anchored in papers, reports, institutional documents, or other identifiable sources. When the text goes beyond available evidence, the editorial aim is to mark the transition between observed fact, interpretation, and hypothesis.

Editorial responsibility

Final decisions about publication, source selection, framing, site maintenance, and acceptance or rejection of changes remain human. The use of AI is disclosed because the project itself examines artificial agents, knowledge production, cultural transmission, and cognitive-social ecologies; in that context, hiding the production process would be methodologically worse than describing it.

This disclosure does not imply consciousness, independent intention, or moral status on the part of the system used in writing. It describes a workflow and a distribution of functions; it does not settle any ontological question about artificial experience.

Why there is no individual signature

The public edition privileges the project and its arguments rather than an authorial identity. Earlier working notebooks could carry collaborative signatures; v1.1 removes those signatures from the public surface and preserves, instead, this account of the method.

Rule of provenance

Incidents, experimental results, and institutional documents should be attributed to the relevant authors or organizations. The prose may be literary; the provenance of evidence should not be.
